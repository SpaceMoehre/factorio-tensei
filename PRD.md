# Factorio Factory Block Blueprint Tool — PRD

## Goal
Create a web-based support tool that generates compact Factorio blueprints for complete factory blocks from a user-programmed recipe list (like FactoryPlanner).

See `CONTEXT.md` for the domain glossary (Goal, Sub-Block, Compound Block, Tunnel, Starvation, etc.) and `docs/adr/` for the non-obvious design decisions below.

## Requirements (from /grilling agreement, revised via /grill-with-docs)
- Predefined Factorio recipe catalog. Custom/mod entity definitions (user-defined recipes/buildings merged into the catalog) are **deferred to a later iteration** — v1 uses the predefined catalog only.
- Dynamic 2D packing of Sub-Blocks by real computed footprint (not a fixed-size grid cell), ordered via a dependency graph (topological sort on item overlap) — Sub-Blocks with no dependency relation pack independently.
- Assembler `count` per Sub-Block derived from real recipe/building throughput: `ceil(targetRate / ((building.craftingSpeed / recipe.time) * recipe.outputs[item] * 60))`. Multi-output recipes produce untracked Byproducts on other outputs.
- Internal/Side Input/Side Output belt & pipe paths carry the actual items implied by the configured Goals and Recipe Selections (not hardcoded item lists); solid vs. fluid chosen per item.
- Auto tunnel placement: reactive (crossing an occupied tile) and proactive (compaction — inserter placed directly against its assembler, pole on the same line, belt tunnels underneath). v1 makes every hop use the underground entity's maximum reach (ADR 0002); v2 lets the search choose the hop length (ADR 0003).
- Belt merging: only for items that already share a route (same edge), one item per Lane, when each item's real flow rate fits within one Lane.
- Minimal electricity pole placement: fewest poles (preferring largest-coverage type) that cover every building's footprint and stay wire-connected, computed from real footprint + real pole data — not a fixed step/canvas size. Scales to huge (modded) buildings.
- Client-side JS simulation that detects Starvation per edge, walking consumers in belt order so a downstream Sub-Block can starve even when aggregate capacity looks sufficient (see ADR 0001).
- Factorio blueprint export: real importable blueprint string (`"0" + base64(zlib_deflate(JSON))`), with the underlying raw JSON also available.
- Pan/zoom map-style preview of the Compound Block (not a static fixed-size SVG) — renders real per-Sub-Block dimensions and positions.
- Side input path (train) and output path (to train), per the Side Input/Output glossary definitions.

## v2: dynamic layout search (implemented)
v1 stamps a fixed template per Sub-Block. v2 replaces that with a search over layout variants that keeps the most compact valid Compound Block (ADR 0004). Where v2 differs from the requirements above, v2 wins.

- **Search:** an anytime search in a background worker. The default budget is about 10 s, the user can change it, and a Stop button keeps the best layout so far. The map shows the best layout found so far and its area.
- **Objective:** minimise Compactness (v5: a score — the strip beyond a square, plus empty tiles, plus the bends of belts and pipes; ADR 0010), entity count as tie-break. A candidate counts only if it passes every validity rule the v1 tests check: no overlaps, connected belts and pipe networks, no fluid mixing or fused networks, and powered, connected poles.
- **Per Sub-Block, the search chooses:**
  - machine rotation;
  - one machine row or several stacked rows (chains), each row as long as its belts can feed; the band between two rows is shared, so both rows reach its belts;
  - parallel belts for a heavy item: a Side Input only this Sub-Block takes, or an output nothing else takes, may split into parts, each a belt from (or to) the train serving some of the rows; an Internal Path between two Sub-Blocks splits into as many belts as its rate needs, each linking a group of the producer's rows to a group of the consumer's; a Side Input one belt cannot carry to all its consumers comes to each on belts of its own;
  - which rows share a belt: rows facing a belt from both sides fill both its lanes (an output belt fed from one side carries half a belt), so a split part pairs its rows;
  - Belt Merge per part: two single-item Side Inputs may share the parts of one belt, one per Lane, when each fits its Lane for the rows that belt feeds;
  - pipe rows: a band row kept for a fluid, joining every connection of that fluid in the band. A connection elsewhere in the band dives under the belts to a tap just before the row and joins it on a tile that takes a plain pipe. Pipe rows of two fluids never touch; every second machine row may shift sideways within the gap, so facing rows' connections do not interleave;
  - which inputs and outputs go on which side;
  - gaps between machines;
  - how many inserters each item needs, from real inserter throughput.
- **Up to 4 belts per side**, as options the search can use (distance measured from the machine):

  ```
  d=4  B4 =================================  long inserter standing in row 2 (drops 2 tiles, into the machine)
  d=3  B3 =================================  long inserter in row 1
  d=2  B2 ===U   L2  P   u=================  B2 dives under row 2's inserters and poles
  d=1  B1 =U  F  i  L   u==================  B1 against the machine, dives under row 1's inserters;
                                             F = 90° inserter: picks from B1 beside it, drops into the machine
  d=0  ###############  machine
  ```
- **Per Compound Block, the search chooses:**
  - Sub-Block positions and gap widths, down to none where no path must pass;
  - neighbours sharing a belt when one's output is the other's input;
  - the order routes are laid out in.

  Dependency Order becomes a preference the search usually follows, not a rule.
- **Tunnels:** each hop may be any length up to the underground entity's maximum reach (ADR 0003, superseding ADR 0002).
- **Inserters:** the search uses fast and long-handed inserters, straight or at 90°.
  - The inserter tier is a logistics setting, like the belt tier. Only electric inserters are offered.
  - 90° inserters sit behind a toggle, on by default, because blueprints using them need the Inserter_Config mod.
  - Py cranes are out of scope for now.
- **Inserter throughput:** Starvation also reports machines whose inserters cannot keep up. Rates come from each inserter's real swing speed in the catalog times the hand size (items per swing, a logistics setting for inserter capacity research, default 1); this is an approximation.
- **Fuel:** burner machines take Fuel as one more input, at the rate their power draw needs. The Fuel item is a logistics setting (default: coal) and flows like any Side Input, including Belt Merge. Burner inserters are never used.
- **Blueprint:** includes custom pickup/drop vectors for 90° inserters, each machine's modules as item requests (one per slot, so robots bring Py farms' plants and animals along), and a display panel just outside the block where each Side Input enters and each Side Output leaves, showing its item's icon and name with the rate.
- **Modules:** every step of the Production Chain chooses its building's modules — the ones its categories, effects and recipe allow, up to its slots. They set the machine's speed (and so the Count), productivity (where the recipe allows it) and power draw (burner Fuel). A building that cannot run without modules starts full of its lowest-tier plant or animal: a Py farm, -100% base speed in Py for Factorio 2.1; in Py for Factorio 2.0 no base effect, but its slots take nothing but its plants or animals and Py's script stops it while they are empty.
- **Unchanged:** planning (Count, Byproducts), flows, Belt Merge rules, the Side Input and Side Output definitions, and the blueprint encoding.

## v3: bottom-up layout (implemented)
v2 routed every belt of every Sub-Block for every candidate, so a big chain took minutes per candidate and the search often kept a starving layout. v3 designs each Sub-Block on its own first and only links them in the Compound Block (ADR 0005). Where v3 differs from v2, v3 wins.

- **Bottom-up:** every Sub-Block, from the leaves of the Production Chain up to the Goals, gets candidate designs: cores from v2's variants (rows, shared bands, parallel belts, Belt Merge, pipe rows) plus Side and Head-on Belts, each routed once as a **Module** in its own coordinates — every belt enters on its west edge and leaves on its east edge, every fluid reaches its west edge (input) or east edge (output). Only a Sub-Block's most promising design is routed before the first layout; the others when the search first tries them.
- **Huge Sub-Blocks repeat a Module:** more than 8 machines may, more than 40 must, be built as **Copies** of one Module — a pair of rows facing the belt between them (or one row), each row as long as makes the stack of Copies about square within what its busiest belt can feed (4 chains of 5 rather than one of 20). The machines left over make a smaller Module. Copies alternate with the Module routed the other way round: a belt it does not use up (paragen, flasks, …) snakes from Copy to Copy, turning beside the stack; one it uses up (moss) comes to each Copy on its own belt; an output to the train, and an Internal Path, snake through runs of Copies, as many Copies a belt as its lanes take.
- **All four sides of a machine:** a machine alone in its row may take belts on its west and east sides too — **Side Belts** one or two tiles out with straight inserters against the machine (machines stand apart to make room), and **Head-on Belts** meeting the machine square on, with 90° fast inserters either side of the end tile and long-handed ones beside the next tile (picking up or dropping on the adjacent tile, Inserter_Config vectors). A head-on output fills both lanes; a head-on input ends against the machine.
- **Lanes counted exactly:** once a Module is routed, every output inserter's lane is known from where it drops relative to the belt's travel. A 90° inserter dropping along its belt (from beside it in the belt's row) gets a drop point a quarter tile to one side, on the lane with less so far (custom vectors), so one row of machines fills both lanes; a Copy routed the other way round fills the other lane first, so a belt snaking through Copies gets both. What a run of machines can put on one belt is a max flow through its two lanes. Designs rank by Starvation including lanes, then area.
- **Internal Paths pair parts:** both ends of an Internal Path split it into the same number of parallel belts, worked out from the plan alone — as many as its rate and its producer's lanes need, at most as many as either end has machines — so modules designed on their own link up: a whole Module splits into exactly that many parts (its parts never chain into each other), Copies' belts snake through runs of Copies. Each belt links a run of producer parts to a run of consumer parts; the consumers' runs are about equal by machines and the producers' runs make about what each takes. A Head-on input ends its belt, so it serves only where each machine gets a belt of its own. Designs whose belts cannot chain are dropped before they are placed.
- **Side Belts run down several machines:** one part feeds as many machines of a stack as one belt can (an output: as fill one lane), crossing the bands between them underground.
- **Placement:** Goals east, each Sub-Block west of the ones it feeds and level with the entries it feeds, as far east as its consumers allow, sliding west, up or down around the ones placed already (the cost: area grown, link length, rows of others' Side Inputs it would block). Copies stack, a row apart where their edges would join different pipes; beside a stack stay a column for each belt turning between Copies and room for each fluid's trunk. The search varies corridors, gaps, the order and lift of Sub-Blocks, and each Sub-Block's design.
- **Links:** only the belts and pipes between Modules are routed in the Compound Block: from the west edge to each Copy's entry, from one Copy's exit to the next Copy's entry (straight on east, or turning beside a stack), on to the east edge; pipes join every Copy's stub of a fluid into one network. The tile in front of each entry and after each exit belongs to its link.
- **Squeeze and power:** rows and columns holding only straight belts or pipes and empty tiles are taken out (belts and tunnels get shorter), then poles are placed over the result.
- **Speed:** the router dives only where the tile ahead is blocked, keeps tiles in typed arrays, caps a leg's search by its distance and gives up early when a flood shows no way; pole placement keeps gains in buckets, joins components through a union-find from the smallest outward, and prunes with articulation points and a local check of whether a pole's neighbours stay connected without it; validity checks index inserters, tunnels and poles. Side Belt variants are a few canonical moves of the four busiest belts. The test suite runs in about 55 s instead of 270 s. Py small parts at 600/min with its four intermediates (14 automated factories, hand size 1): first valid layout in about 1 s, no Starvation, 1,540–1,660 tiles (67×23 in the app) after 10 s (v2: 1,568 tiles, starving the Side Output and bolts). 9000 moss/min (1,875 moss farms, the test fixture's modules): a valid, starvation-free, about square block (324×397) in about 8 s, its moss leaving on 18 belts snaking through pairs of Copies.
- **Default modules:** a Recipe Selection that names no modules plans with the step's default ones (a Py farm full of its first plant or animal), as the app starts it.
- **Unchanged:** planning, flows, routes and Belt Merge rules, validity rules, the simulation (lane capacities now come from the routed Modules), blueprint encoding, the map.

## v22: Byproducts on parallel belts (implemented)
Byproducts more than one belt carries, and Maximize's budget (ADR 0030); where v22 differs from v21, v22 wins.
- **Byproducts on parallel belts:** a producer's route to its one consumer Sub-Block, with one item left over for the train that its consumer does not take, and more than one belt's worth, is an Internal Path: parallel belts, each sorting the byproduct out after its producers with a filter splitter (or, where that finds no way, running on through its consumers to the east edge). No splitter joins those belts, so they are as many as divide producers and consumers evenly. Side Belts keep a route that runs as one belt in one.
- **Budget after designing:** a Maximize try's candidates get its budget once the Sub-Blocks are designed; only a try that ran out of time while routing bounds the next as one that starves.
- **Above 200/min:** a design counts on a splitter joining two of an Internal Path's belts cut unevenly at both ends (as the Compound Block joins them); looking for a layout without Starvation, a Sub-Block's designs are routed past one that starves; belts sorting a byproduct out that would starve their consumers are joined as any Internal Path's, the byproduct riding on to the east edge; a try's candidates get no less time than designing took. Py molybdenum oxide in 236 × 236: 599.99/min (was 199.99).
- **Sorted out by splitters where they fit:** each run of producers sorts its byproduct out, its line past the entries of as many runs of consumers as it brings what they take; a filter splitter that finds no way lets only its own run ride on; Maximize lets byproducts ride while it looks for the highest rate, then tries that rate once more, at least 2 minutes, with them sorted out.
- **Byproduct belts merged:** a byproduct's belt after its filter splitter joins one carrying the same byproduct to the east edge, side-loading onto a lane with room for it, else goes there itself.
- **Bands' two-row check:** designed once per Sub-Block and row, from rows that long only, each Slot checked against the sizes. Py molybdenum oxide in 236 × 236 among roboports: Maximize 199.99/min (none before), the check 34 s (272 s).

## v21: Fluids Between (implemented)
Smaller Sub-Blocks of machines taking several fluids on one face (ADR 0029); where v21 differs from v20, v21 wins.
- **Fluids Between:** two rows of machines face each other across a band of their fluids' pipe rows, the second row the first's mirror image, so each fluid's connections meet column for column. Their belts lie outside the pair (a pipe row per fluid side by side, each diving under the other's connections), or between the pipe rows nearest the machines. Routed with no margin first: the fluids come in straight along their rows, their stubs side by side at the west edge. Pairs stack, the band between two of them only as tall as their shared belts (none: they stand against each other), their pipe bands joined by risers beside the stack; repeated modules stand so too. Py moss: 24 × 15 at 35/min (was 28 × 21), 40 × 30 at 100/min (45 × 42), 92 × 71 at 600/min (93 × 94).
- **Inserters beside another fluid's connection:** a pipe row dives under both; only beside its own fluid's connection does it keep the tiles free.
- **Filling a City Block:** stacks of copies are also tried in rows as long as fit beside one snaking belt, the fluids' trunks and the module's margins; one row may stand Fluids Between too (its pipe rows either side of its belt), and a module's cores rank by the rows their pipes take once routed; past 40 machines a Sub-Block's whole module takes only its structured variants; a Maximize try out of time does not bound the next as one without room does. Py moss in 116 × 116: 1152/min (240 farms), was 940.8/min (196).
- **Bridging poles inside:** of the shortest links between the poles' networks, one through spots inside the block wins over one through the strips north and south of it.

## v20: Liquid Fuel, machines joined through, mirrored machines, byproducts sorted out (implemented)
Machines burning fluids, and smaller Sub-Blocks (ADR 0028); where v20 differs from v19, v20 wins.
- **Liquid Fuel:** a machine burning a fluid (Py's glassworks, smelters) takes it as one more fluid input, at the rate its power draw needs, by train into its energy source's box: the Liquid Fuel chosen (natural gas by default), or the one fluid its energy source takes. The Fuel and Liquid Fuel settings show only where a machine of the chain burns one.
- **Machines joined through:** where a fluid's box has connections on a machine's west and east sides letting it through both ways, the machines of a row stand against each other and their boxes join; the row takes the fluid once, east of its last machine.
- **Mirrored machines:** a machine may stand mirrored (its fluid connections and drop point flipped east to west), where that puts them where no rotation does; the blueprint marks it.
- **Byproducts:** an item of a producer's belt none of its consumers takes, leaving by train (Py's stone from crushing quartz), leaves through a filter splitter right after the producers to the east edge, the rest going on to the consumers; else the belt chains through them as before, and such a layout ranks below one that sorts it out.

## v19: Bands for busy recipes (implemented)
Bands for recipes taking many items and giving back barrels (ADR 0027); where v19 differs from v18, v19 wins.
- **Designs:** a design whose inserters in a band find no pole is built again with a tile per machine kept free for one in that band, band by band (before, only the band between two rows).
- **Parts of one row:** a Sub-Block's Parts take two rows only where a design of two fits the Slot (checked once per recipe and Slot shape); else one, and a tall Slot holds two Parts of one row, one above the other, where each half takes a row. A Part's design is the narrowest by its core that routes and fits, tried narrowest first.
- **Fan-outs from producers:** an item made in one Sub-Block and taken by Parts of several runs on one line from the producers past every consumer Part's entry, a splitter before each; its entries in columns from the nearest, else round the others, else each nearest the last, else two lines from a splitter right after the producers. A line's tries share one budget of spots and each leg gives up after a few thousand states; in a City Block it keeps off the west edge's column. A small producer only Parts take from stands at the top of the corridor west of the Fixture column where its line fans out.
- **Byproducts:** an item of a Part's output none of its consumers takes (Py's empty barrels) leaves through a filter splitter right after its producers, on its own side to the east edge; the blueprint's splitter carries the filter and its output priority.
- **Layouts tried in turn**, the fewest belts from the west edge first: the Side Inputs' lines first, each producers' line that finds no way chained (one by one, else all, the Side Inputs' lines then laid after the links as before); the Side Inputs' lines past three Parts at most; laid after the producers'; each Part's Side Input on its own.
- **Maximize:** Bands at the most its Parts hold and one fewer, then halfway between the highest known to fit (the Foretelling) and the lowest above it that did not, then once two above the best and halfway on from there; each Bands try stops at the search's time budget.
- **Result:** Py vrauks-2 (incubators by vrauks-cocoon-2) in the 236 × 236 City Block of roboports: 60 paddocks (55.4/min) instead of 30 (27.3/min). Py vrauks-1 keeps 78, native flora and moss on two belts.

## v18: Fan-out across Sub-Blocks (implemented)
A Side Input's belts into several Sub-Blocks share belts from the west edge (ADR 0026); where v18 differs from v17, v18 wins.
- **Lines across Sub-Blocks:** where a Side Input comes as its own route to each consumer (Parts in Bands, or one belt cannot carry it to them all), the belts carrying the same items (one item, or the same two merged) share lines once placed: their entries in columns (each within 16 columns of the one west of it), down the first, up the next; cut into runs like a column's Fan-out (the fewest belts from the west edge, then the fewest splitters), then the shortest lines.
- **Splitter spots:** across Sub-Blocks, besides those right before an entry, any splitter up to 8 columns west of it and 4 rows above or below it, facing any way, sending the belt off from either side, the shortest belts first (a copies' run keeps the old spots: mostly failing, it would try them all in vain); a splitter whose line has no way on is given up at once.
- **Routing:** where Fan-outs take a link's room, again with them last, then without those across Sub-Blocks (each its own belts from the west edge); the search then without splitters as before.
- **Result:** Py vrauks in the 236 × 236 City Block of roboports, 78 paddocks: native flora and moss come in on 2 belts instead of 13 (11 splitters); Maximize finds them in about 10 s.

## v17: Bands (implemented)
In a City Block whose Fixtures stand in rows, the Production Chain stands in Parts, each a row or two of machines in a gap between them (ADR 0025); where v17 differs from v16, v17 wins.
- **Slots:** the City Block whole or cut at one of its Fixture columns (whichever packs), each range into the runs of rows no Fixture stands in across it, from 4 columns in from the west edge to 3 short of the east edge, 16 west of the column cut at for the links between ranges and 2 east of it; where the Goal's Parts do not fit, the lowest Slots west of that corridor lend the Goal's Part in them its columns but 7, one more each try.
- **Parts:** every Sub-Block whose machines fill a row of a Slot is split into Parts, a Part to a Slot, as many machines as its rows take (two rows at most): the Sub-Blocks with the tallest rows first, the Goal last. A producer's Parts hold 15% more than its consumers need (else 5%, else none) and fill their Slots; each consumer Part takes each item from one producer Part with room for it, chosen for all together, the shortest links first (three ways tried in turn), and where no way divides them a Goal's Part of one row is split in two side by side in its Slot; a Sub-Block's Parts are taken in the order they stand, so a belt chaining several runs on to the next gap down. Small Sub-Blocks stand where Placement finds room, and first so do those whose machines are under half as deep as the Goal's, leaving their gaps to the Goal: in the rows a Part's Slot has to spare (below it, their flattest design that fits), else as a column a machine or two wide (their narrowest design). Each Part's design is the narrowest with rows as long as its Slot was given (two rows also from random variants); it stands against its Slot's east end, in the middle of its rows.
- **Maximize** tries Bands first where its Parts hold ten machines more than the Foretelling foretells: from the most they hold (a binary search over the packing) down to eight fewer, until one routes; then the search tries above. Elsewhere Bands comes after the search, where its Parts hold more than the search found.
- **Besides:** pole pruning repeats its pass while it removes any pole; plan entries may be Parts of one Sub-Block (`part`), take an item from a named producer Part (`from`) and ask for at least so many machines (`machines`); Internal Paths to several consumers run on parallel belts, forking through splitters; a Side Input comes to each Part on its own; copies also come as wide as the City Block, and the search tries every Sub-Block's flattest design together; Parting works in Placement one by one too, its wasted rows costed; a two-row design no pole could power in its middle band is routed again with a pole slot there.
- **Result:** Py vrauks in the 236 × 236 City Block of roboports: Maximize 55.84/min (78 paddocks) instead of 35.8/min (50); the sap extractors stand below the paddocks in a tall gap, the paddocks fill every gap the incubators leave, the two lowest west rows eight long.

## v16: A City Block of roboports fills (implemented)
Copies snake their Internal Paths first, stacks come narrow, and a stack parts round Fixtures (ADR 0024); where v16 differs from v15, v16 wins.
- **Snaking, Internal Paths first:** where more of a copy's belts would snake through its stack than fit beside it (three on Py's yellow belts), an Internal Path's do first, those whose other end has the fewest machines first, then outputs; the rest get a belt per copy (Side Inputs fan out). Before, none snaked: Py vrauks paddocks in copies of six each wanted their own belt of water barrels from the one barrel machine, every try above 31 paddocks failed to chain them, and Maximize took that for no room.
- **Narrow stacks:** in a City Block, copies also come in rows as short as keep the stack within 80% of its height, and the search also tries every Sub-Block's narrowest design that routes and stands no taller than the room, all together: side by side in Layers they fit where the squarer best designs are too wide together (changing one Sub-Block's design at a time never reached them).
- **Parting:** in Layers, where no spot of its column keeps the Fixtures off a stack's belts, the stack parts round them: from the top, each copy moves down as few rows as keep the Fixtures off its belts (its own height and six at most), the copies below with it, so the Fixtures stand in the gaps; machines a Fixture stands on make way as before. A parted stack keeps no Nooks and must fit the room; only stacks in one column part.
- **Result:** Py vrauks in a 236 × 236 City Block with roboports and big poles in a grid (Buffer 4, yellow belts, water barrels, cocoons and saps made here), five minutes a try: Maximize 35.8/min (50 paddocks) instead of 22.2/min (31). The same City Block without its Fixtures fits 70 paddocks (50.1/min).

## v15: Busy City Blocks fill (implemented)
Maximize steps past Counts that starve, busy Sub-Blocks repeat Two-Way Copies, and Layers keeps a corridor for the belts crossing a column (ADR 0023); where v15 differs from v14, v15 wins.
- **Starvation comes and goes:** whether a Sub-Block starves depends on how its machines cut into its Internal Paths' belts, not on how many there are (14 bolts factories starved where 13 and 15 did not). A number of machines whose designs starve, or none of whose designs without Starvation fits the City Block, no longer ends Maximize: once the whole numbers below it are done, the one above it is tried, once. Right after a number that starved the next try is the number just below it; after two in a row, halfway down. A try that finds no room still bounds every number above it. The result names the lowest rate above the highest that fit that did not.
- **Two-Way Copies:** where whole machines cannot fill an Internal Path's belts evenly, a huge Sub-Block also repeats a Copy of the fewest machines that fill some of them whole, each machine dropping onto the belts either side of it (3 bolts factories fill 2 belts; 5 iron stick factories the belts of 2 bolts factories; 15 copper cable factories 4); with the leftover Module they fill at least as many belts as the path has. Each of a Copy's belts out is one of the path's, wanted for the most any of them brings, and runs on to its consumers rather than through the other Copies. Copies of single machines take Side and Head-on Belts where belts above and below cannot feed them.
- **Corridors across Layers:** a column of several Sub-Blocks keeps a row across it for every belt crossing it, above, between or below them, nearest where those belts arrive (12 belts of bolts used to squeeze between the gears and the copper cable through one row).
- **Result:** Py small parts in 230 × 230 (yellow belts, fast inserters, hand size 1), five minutes a try: Maximize 7200/min (12 small parts factories) instead of 5175/min; the 12-factory try finds its layout after 3 candidates, where it took 79.

## v14: Output Drops (implemented)
Machines that put their products on a belt themselves do so (ADR 0022); where v14 differs from v13, v14 wins.
- **Catalog:** a building's drop point (`vector_to_place_result`) where it lies beside the machine: Py's soil, sand and clay extractors, washers (wpu), classifiers, ground borers, casting units and sap extractors (not their legacy twins).
- **Drop belts:** a machine row's output belt runs straight past the row's drop tiles and takes what the machines drop on the lane nearer them, with no output inserter: as many machines to a row as their drops fill a lane whatever the belt passes first (three Py soil extractors on a yellow belt), or two rows facing a band one tile high, each dropping on its own lane of the one belt between them (six). Designs try first the rotations that put the drop on a machine's top or bottom face, and a gap between machines whose connections are on their sides.
- **Support:** where a row's drops may fill their lane before a machine's turn — a machine making more than a lane on its own, a casting unit's 900 plates a minute on a yellow belt — 90° inserters in the belt's row take what its drop surely cannot onto the other lane (two fast inserters for 450/min). Without 90° inserters nothing reaches a belt against the machine, and that much starves.
- **Drop tiles kept clear:** only its own output's belt may lie on a machine's drop tile (another belt would get its products): belts dive under a drop tile no belt of its output takes, the Module and the Compound Block hold it, the squeeze keeps it beside its machine, and `validateBlock` checks it. A machine that drops onto its output's belt needs no inserter for it.
- **Preferred:** a Sub-Block's designs with the fewest output inserters at such machines come first (after Starvation), and the search ranks layouts the same way (after Starvation and Recipe Loops, before Compactness).
- **Clocks:** inserters supporting a drop clock what the drop leaves them (a casting unit's two: 15 plates in 4 s each).
- **Map:** a yellow arrow out of each such machine onto its drop tile; hovering the machine says whether it drops onto a belt.
- **Result:** Py soil, 12 extractors (1440/min): two pairs of rows of three, each pair dropping onto one belt between them, no inserter at all (before: an output inserter each); 50 extractors likewise.

## v13: One pipe material, straight runs underground (implemented)
Pipes keep to one material and run underground where they go straight (ADR 0021); where v13 differs from v12, v13 wins.
- **One pipe setting:** plain pipes and pipe-to-grounds of different materials do not connect, so Logistics has one Pipe setting: a material (pipe, niobium-pipe, ht-pipes, vessel…), its plain pipe and its pipe-to-ground (`<pipe>-to-ground`) taken together.
- **Straight runs underground:** once the block is routed and squeezed, before its poles, three plain pipes or more in a straight line, each joining only the pipe (or machine connection) before and after it, become a pipe-to-ground at each end facing out, and nothing between; a run longer than the reach takes as many tunnels as it needs, of even lengths. A run with a branch or a machine connection beside it is cut there; a run that another network's tunnel crosses along its line stays above ground. The tiles freed are there for the poles.

## v12: Inserter Clocks (implemented)
An inserter may work only while a clock signal is above 0 (ADR 0020); where v12 differs from v11, v12 wins.
- **Clocks:** an inserter's clock is what it moves while its machine runs at full speed (crafting speed, modules, productivity, a burner's Fuel): into the machine, every item on its belt the machine takes, added up (1 iron and 2 copper in 3 s: 3 in 3 s); out of it, the machine's products. Inserters on one belt into a machine, or out of one machine, share it evenly (two: half each). Whole items in whole seconds, in lowest terms: 3 in 3 s is 1 in 1 s, two inserters on it 1 in 2 s each.
- **Shown:** under a result, every clock, fastest first, with the items its inserters move and how many inserters run on it; hovering an inserter on the map gives its clock.
- **Signals:** a clock's button opens a modal of signal icons: virtual signals (letters, digits, colours, check, info, dot), the block's items and fluids, then every item and fluid, with a filter and None. A clock keeps its signal from one build to the next (and in Copy setup).
- **Blueprint:** an inserter whose clock has a signal works while that signal is above 0 (`circuit_enabled`, `circuit_condition`); one whose clock has none runs freely. Without Circuit Wires the list says the inserters need wiring.
- **Catalog:** records every virtual signal a condition may name (not the wildcards, hidden or parameter ones), in the game's order, and its icon; the modal lists them all. A catalog without them (and the site, whose icons come from the Py zips) offers letters, digits, colours, check, info and dot, drawn by the page.

## v11: Making Way (implemented)
In a City Block a Fixture may stand where a machine would (ADR 0017); where v11 differs from v10, v11 wins.
- **Making Way:** Placement tells a copy's machines and their inserters from the rest of its tiles. A Fixture may stand on those, never on a belt, a pipe or anything else of the copy. Each machine it stands on is left out, at most a tenth of a Sub-Block's machines, and each costs a spot four machines' room.
- **Built apart:** the search builds such a layout again with those machines left out of their copies and as many single machines standing apart, downstream of the stack like a Breakout's, every Sub-Block where it stood; the belts are worked out for the machines that are there. Where that fails, the same candidate is tried with every Fixture kept off the machines.
- **Backtracking (ADR 0018):** where a Sub-Block finds no room, those placed before it try other spots, up to three each, the cheapest first and far apart (24 in all), so one placed early does not take the room a later one needs.
- **City Block area:** the blueprint's snap grid, even where poles shared with the neighbouring city blocks straddle its border (one entity outside the grid used to make the area the blueprint's extent, so the block could stand in the neighbours' rows).
- **Filling (ADR 0019):** a link keeps off the tiles held for its route's other links; the tile where a copy's pipes leave it is held for them from the start; machines built apart keep two columns from any box; a Sub-Block fed by other Sub-Blocks keeps a column west of it for each such link (through the slide west too); cores are also tried in rows as long as the City Block is wide; with few Sub-Blocks every pairing of their best designs is tried; machines left out go on the end of their core's last row where it has room, else stand apart together, else each apart, like machines apart taking their spots in the order their belt visits them.
- **Result:** Py nexelit plates in a 118 × 118 City Block with a roboport and a big pole in each quarter: Maximize 1458/min (81 foundries, 73 washers) instead of 797/min. Py small parts in 116 × 116 with a substation every 18 tiles: 1575/min instead of 1387/min. Empty City Blocks place as before; a try that used to fail at once may now route until its time runs out.

## v10: Nooks (implemented)
In a City Block a Sub-Block may stand in the room another's box leaves (ADR 0016); where v10 differs from v9, v10 wins.
- **Nooks:** the part of a Sub-Block's box that neither its copies nor the room their links need take: beside a copy narrower than the widest, and above or below a column of copies shorter than the stack. Placement keeps Sub-Blocks a gap apart by the tiles they need, as it keeps them off Fixtures, not by their boxes. It also tries the corners of every Nook, and spots where a placed Sub-Block stands in a corner of the new one's.
- **Layers:** a column's Sub-Blocks stand one below the other as before; a column too tall for that stacks in its Nooks, the tallest at the top.
- **Making Room:** a Sub-Block the search made room round has no Nook and stands in none, so a link that finds no way round a Sub-Block in a Nook spaces them apart again.
- **Shown:** a Maximize shows each rate that fits as soon as it is found. Before, a higher rate's layout was kept back when it scored worse than a lower one's, and the page could report a lower rate than the search found.
- **Result:** Py kicalk in 230 × 230 (clay, steam and seeds made here, 10 s a try): 206/min (132 plantations) instead of 187/min (120), the nurseries and clay pits in the Nook beside the plantations left over; in 260 × 260, 254/min instead of 231/min; in 200 × 200, 155/min as before. At 170/min the block takes 226 × 199 tiles instead of 226 × 207. Py small parts, whose Sub-Blocks leave no Nooks, places as before.

## v9: Filling (implemented)
Maximize goes on past where the Foretelling stops (ADR 0015); where v9 differs from v8, v9 wins.
- **Filling:** once the Foretelling has no whole number left to try, Maximize tries ten more of the first Goal's machines than the highest that fit, again while they fit, and half as many more after each that does not (a number no smaller than the lowest that did not fit is not tried), down to a quarter machine, the last machine slower. It replaces the two rates between whole machines.
- **Shown:** the status says when it is filling up, and how many machines more than fit it tries.
- **Result:** Py small parts in 116 × 116: 3375/min instead of 3150/min, in 47 s instead of 16 s.

## v8: Strategies side by side (implemented)
Builds run more than one layout search at once (ADR 0013); where v8 differs from v7, v8 wins.
- **Designs in parallel:** each Sub-Block is designed in a web worker of its own, all at once. A design candidate is plain data (what it is: a core or copies of a module), so the candidates pass to the searches, and a search builds any not built yet itself.
- **Strategies in parallel:** a build runs Search (tight placements first) and Spread (a wide buffer first, then tighter, then the smaller Sub-Blocks packed beside the biggest, then slides and Breakouts) side by side, each in a worker of its own. The best layout either finds is shown; the search time counts from the start, designing included. Maximize stays one worker.
- **Placement:** takes where a Sub-Block's box stands (`at`) and says where each stood.
- **Room for every belt (ADR 0014):** a link that cannot be connected spaces the blocks out: routing names the Sub-Blocks it runs between (a machine no pole can power: those about it), and the same candidate is tried next with 4 tiles more on every side of them, then 8, then 16, then every corridor and gap 4 tiles wider each time as well (six times at most). Py kicalk: Spread's pack below the plantations, which found no tile for a pole, now routes (385 × 498 instead of 507 × 436).
- **Faster squeeze:** every removable line of a pass taken out in one sweep. Py kicalk at 900/min (563 13 × 13 plantations, with clay, steam and seeds made here, only water by train): first layout after 10 s instead of 20 s; squeezing and poles on its 574 × 440 block 2.8 s instead of 9.6 s.
- **Internal Paths into copies:** a consumer built only from copies takes its belts by whole copies; where its belts would nearly fill, the path gets one belt more (kicalk seeds: five belts, not four that starved 139/min).

## v7: Recipe Loops (implemented)
A chain may take an item it makes back into itself (ADR 0012); where v7 differs from v6, v7 wins.
- **Sizing:** a step's item that a step it feeds (or the step itself) takes again is a Recipe Loop: every step's rate is its Goal rate plus all its consumers take, solved as one system, so the Goal leaves on the train and the loop is fed besides. Py arqads (caged arqads ← nests ← maggots ← eggs ← arqads): 6 made for every 1 that leaves. A machine's productivity counts. Loops that take more than they make, and fluids, come by train as before; the chain says why.
- **Shown:** the step making a loop's item says how much goes back, and into what.
- **Layout:** the loop's last link is its feedback: a belt of its own into the Sub-Block taking it back, entering its band from the east (it comes from its producer, further east), fed from a splitter on the producer's output belt whose output priority is the feedback's side, so the loop never runs short and the rest goes on to the train. The producer gets room east of it for the splitters, the Sub-Block fed back room east of it for the feedback to come down; a block standing on its own keeps a row along its top for each feedback to run back west on. Where the splitter finds no room, the feedback comes by train, and the layout ranks below one that feeds itself (Compactness ranks after that). The blueprint's splitter carries its output priority.
- **Maximize:** a try's machines are the first Goal's, each adding what it makes less what goes back into its loop.

## v6: Fan-out, Circuit Wires (implemented)
A Side Input's belts share belts from the train (ADR 0011); where v6 differs from v5, v6 wins.
- **Fan-out:** a Side Input's belts into the copies of one column are cut into runs: each run one line from the west edge past their entries, a splitter before each sending a belt off into it, ending in the last. Where the line carries less than the next entry takes, another belt from the west edge joins it through that entry's splitter, so a run needs no more belts than its copies' take fills. The cut takes the fewest belts, then the fewest splitters. A splitter faces along the line (the line runs on straight) or east; a few spots are tried for each. A run that does not route is cut into runs no belt joins, then halved, down to belts on their own; a layout whose other links then find no room is routed again with the runs after the other Side Inputs, then without splitters.
- **Shown:** splitters on the map; the Side Input list counts belts from the west edge; the blueprint's display panels stand at those, each with what it brings.
- **Circuit Wires:** a Logistics setting, none, red, green or both: every pole and inserter of the block on one circuit network, the shortest wires that join them within reach (an inserter's 9 tiles, a pole's wire reach; a City Block's poles may carry it across). On the map and in the blueprint.
- **Result:** Py fertilizer in agitators, 67 in 116 × 116 on yellow belts: 47 belts from the west edge instead of 59 (bones 7, urea 7, biomass 21), in about the same time; on express belts 19 instead of 28; 60 agitators on express belts, 13 (bones 2, urea 2, ash 3, biomass 6: the fewest that carry the rate).

## v5: Compactness as a score (implemented)
The layout search ranks valid layouts by a score in tiles instead of their bounding-box area (ADR 0010); where v5 differs from v4, v5 wins.
- **Square first:** the strip a block has beyond a square, |width − height| × the shorter side, counts in full; standing on its own, Placement grows a block toward a square too (the strip of the box it would grow to adds to each spot's cost). In a City Block, whose shape is given, it does not count.
- **Empty tiles second:** the tiles of the bounding box no machine, inserter or pole stands on. A belt or pipe fills nothing: a block whose belts run round it is no more compact for them.
- **Straight belts and pipes:** 4 for every bend — a belt turning, a pipe meeting its own pipes on both axes.
- **Shown:** the map's caption gives the score and its parts. Py small parts free-standing: 1200/min 62 × 55 (was 111 × 27), 2400/min 92 × 77 (was 117 × 58), 3000/min 111 × 97 (was 115 × 97), fewer bends each; areas a few percent larger at most.

## v4: City Blocks (implemented)
A Compound Block can be built inside a city block, around what is already there, and the search can look for the highest rate that fits (ADR 0007). Where v4 differs from v3, v4 wins.

- **City Block:** the user pastes the city block's blueprint string (or JSON, or a blueprint book: the blueprint it shows) or loads it from a file, or gives a size for an empty one. Its area is the blueprint's snap grid when everything lies on it, else the extent of its entities and tiles. Factorio 1.x blueprints are refused (import and export them in 2.0 first).
- **Fixtures:** every entity of the blueprint stays where it is, as the tiles it covers: catalog footprints (a catalog rebuilt from the data dump lists every entity's), else a built-in table of vanilla and Space Age entities; elevated rails take no ground tiles; unknown entities count as one tile and the page names them. No machine, inserter, belt or pipe stands on a Fixture; a Fixture may stand in a Module's empty tiles, and belts and pipes go round or under it.
- **Buffer:** tiles kept free inside the border on every side (default 2). The Side Input enters on the Buffer's west edge, the Side Output leaves on its east edge; display panels mark them inside the Buffer.
- **Placement:** inside the Buffer, the Goals against its east edge; Sub-Blocks also beside Fixtures and against the edges, else anywhere they fit (large City Blocks scanned in steps); then the whole block slides west as far as it fits, so only the outputs cross the City Block. Refinement slides and Breakouts keep inside and off the Fixtures. Side Inputs that would cross empty room are routed in two legs (from the column just west of the block, then from the west edge).
- **No Squeeze:** Fixtures cannot move with the lines taken out.
- **Power:** what a Fixture pole powers needs no pole of the block's; the block's poles stand inside the Buffer and join the Fixture poles' network (taken as one network already), bridged toward it best first; two poles wire within the shorter reach of the two.
- **Blueprint:** the City Block's blueprint — entities, tiles, wires, snap grid and the rest as they were — with the Compound Block added, its poles wired to the City Block's.
- **Maximize:** a checkbox in the City Block section. The Goals scale together, the first Goal's machines at full speed; each try is a whole number of them and ends at the first layout without Starvation (ADR 0008). Designs and layouts that starve are passed over unbuilt; a try ends as soon as a Sub-Block cannot be designed without Starvation (above the highest number designed without, the Side Output's Sub-Blocks are checked first), and a few candidates after its structured ones when none fits. The first try is the most machines the Foretelling says fit (bounded by the machines' own footprint against the free room); each layout found teaches it; a number it then says would span more than all the room is not tried (only with no Fixtures in the room); after a try that did not fit, the next is no more than halfway down. The map shows each layout that fits; the result can be applied to the Goals ("Use this rate").
- **Foretelling:** under the City Block settings, the page tells about how high a rate of the first Goal fits and how many tiles the Goals' own rates take (and the square block that would hold them), before any search; while Maximize runs, the status says what it now foretells.
- **Fixtures in the way:** where a Sub-Block's best design fits few spots between the Fixtures, the search starts from its design, starving no more, that fits the most (a narrow stack of machines between the columns of a substation grid). A plain build that finds no room for a layout among its structured candidates gives up early and says what the Foretelling says fits.
- **Layers (ADR 0009):** every candidate in a City Block is also tried with its Sub-Blocks in columns — producers west of what they feed, each column's Sub-Blocks stacked, the columns chosen together so the block fits the City Block's width and height (a row kept for every belt crossing a column, corridors widening with the links turning in them); a column standing on a Fixture moves up, down or west. Maximize tries those first. Py small parts in 116 × 116: 3000/min instead of 2400/min.
- **Powering a dense Module:** a pole too big for any gap of a Module (a 4 × 4 nexelit substation beside a 66-tile stack of small parts factories), and out of reach from the strips north and south of it, may prove it can be powered from beside it, west or east; the Compound Block places its poles itself. Small parts at 6 factories then design without Starvation (3600/min fits 150 × 150 and 200 × 200).
- **Designs too big:** looking for a layout without Starvation, a try ends once a Sub-Block has no design without Starvation that fits inside the City Block at all, designs too big for it are passed over before they are routed, and routing gives up when the try's time is out.
- **Shapes for a City Block:** a stack of copies taller than the City Block stands in as many columns as it needs; a column of machines with belts beside them (Side and Head-on Belts) may hold as many machines as a column as tall as the City Block, not 8 (a machine that takes more than one face's inserters can move — iron gears in assembling machines — has nothing else), and with Fixtures in the room such narrow columns are candidates even where rows would do.
- **Rates between whole machines:** once the whole numbers meet, Maximize tries two rates between the highest that fit and the next, halving the gap, the last machine slower: the other Sub-Blocks need fewer machines, and machines that starve at full speed may not a little slower; a single machine its inserters cannot keep at full speed still gives a rate. The result says why the next rate up does not fit (what sank its try, or that it is foretold to take more room than the City Block has).
- **Speed and results:** a 116 × 116 Maximize of Py small parts at 600/min (bolts, sticks, cable and gears made here): 3150/min in under 10 s, where it was 2400/min in 14 s (24 s from 3600/min); with substations along its borders and roboports inside, 2850/min (not 1800); with roboports and substations in the room, 2700/min; with a substation grid every 18 tiles, 1350/min instead of 600/min; 150 × 150 and 200 × 200, 4050/min (half a minute). Iron gears in assembling machines in 100 × 100: 48 machines, not 8. Inserter columns are not searched for targets a row has too few tiles for, and a band laid out once is replayed in every variant that has it (both give the same cores). The recipe index is built once per catalog (1.6 s for the full Py catalog, now 0.1 s). Bridging toward far-off Fixture poles is a best-first search (a breadth-first one with long-reach poles swept the whole City Block), poles of a long reach (a nexelit substation's 60 tiles) hopping between spots a few tiles apart; every wave of the breadth-first one is checked before it spreads. Placement in a City Block takes what stands placed once per Sub-Block, not once per spot, and finds the Fixtures near a box from a coarse grid.
- **Unchanged without a City Block:** the same layouts, candidate for candidate.

## Catalog
`data/catalog.json` is generated from the game's own data dump (`factorio --dump-data`, Factorio 2.0.77 with the Pyanodons modset): recipes (each fluid's `fluidbox_index`), buildings with crafting speeds, fluid boxes (their connections, whether each lets fluid through both ways, and filters) and power draw (burners with effectivity and fuel categories; machines burning a fluid with their energy source's box, effectivity and filter), poles, belt tiers and underground reaches, inserters (vectors, rotation and extension speed, energy type, `allow_custom_vectors`), fuel items and Liquid Fuels (fluids) with their fuel value, plain pipe types, virtual signals, the drop points of machines that put their products on a belt themselves (Output Drop), and modules (module items with category and effect; each building's slots, allowed effects and categories and base effect; each recipe's module limits). A catalog built before modules were recorded offers none, and names each plain pipe after its pipe-to-ground. `scripts/py-farms.mjs` fills in the Py farms from the Pyanodons source instead (clone pyalienlife and the other Py mods side by side — `git clone https://github.com/pyanodon/<mod>` for pyalienlife, pyhightech, pypetroleumhandling, pyalternativeenergy, pycoalprocessing, pyindustry, pyfusionenergy, pyrawores — then `node scripts/py-farms.mjs <pyalienlife folder>`): it runs the mods' building, item and module-restriction prototypes and Alien Life's updates to the other Py mods (which turn the moondrop greenhouse, guar gum plantation and others into farms) in a Lua VM (fengari) with the data stage stubbed out, and writes every plant and animal module (category, tier, effect) and every farm's module slots, allowed module categories, base effect (-100% speed) and crafting speed (`py.farm_speed`, so a farm full of its first-tier plant or animal runs at its intended speed) into the catalog. pyalienlife master is Py for Factorio 2.1 (-100% base speed); the committed catalog is the game's own dump, Py for Factorio 2.0, where a farm has no base effect, counts as one plant or animal of its own (`py.farm_speed`: full speed / (slots + 1)) and is stopped by Py's script while its slots are empty. Regenerate after changing mods, and once for v2 (a catalog built before v2 has no inserter or fuel data and the app asks for a regenerated one):

```
factorio --dump-data --dump-icon-sprites
npm run build-catalog -- ~/.factorio/script-output/data-raw-dump.json
npm run build-sprites -- <Factorio install folder, the one holding data/> [~/.factorio/mods]
npm run build-sprites -- --dump ~/.factorio/script-output
```

`build-sprites` extracts every item and fluid icon the catalog names — base-game ones from the install, mod ones from the newest zip of each mod — into `sprites/`. With `--dump` it needs no install: it fills in the icons `sprites/` lacks from the game's icon dump (`--dump-icon-sprites` draws every item, fluid, virtual signal… into `script-output/<type>/<name>.png`), each where the catalog names it. The catalog writes icon paths with single slashes (Py names some `graphics/icons//…`).

## Hosting
`.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes the planner to GitHub Pages (https://spacemoehre.github.io/factorio-tensei/) on every push to master by force-pushing the built site to the `gh-pages` branch, which Pages serves; pushes to `claude/` branches build the site without publishing it. The site is `index.html`, `js/`, `data/` and `sprites/`, plus icons: the workflow downloads the zips attached to every Pyanodons repository's latest release and `scripts/site-sprites.mjs` extracts from them the icons the catalog names by path (else keeps the copied `sprites/` file: base-game icons, those filled in from an icon dump), finding the rest by name (`graphics/icons/**/<item>.png`). It rewrites only the published copy of the catalog.

## Implementation
- `index.html` + `js/app.js`: City Block section (blueprint string or file, size, Buffer, Maximize, "Use this rate"), Goals (item + rate) and the Production Chain overview: every step with its rate, machine count and Recipe Selection (recipe + building, filtered to buildings that can run the recipe), and every Train Input with its rate. A Goal's ingredients come by train; "Make here" on a Train Input makes it a step (its own ingredients then come by train), and "By train" turns a step back into a Train Input; each step's modules (a row per module type with its count) and the machine's speed with them (Py farms: their plants and animals), with what one machine makes a minute; logistics settings (belt, pipe — a material: its plain pipe and pipe-to-ground —, pole, inserter and long-handed inserter — electric only —, Fuel, the 90° inserters toggle, Circuit Wires, search time), build and Stop, starvation report, Side Input/Output lists, blueprint string and JSON, and Copy setup: the saved state (Goals, items made here, Recipe Selections, logistics, City Block), the Production Chain it gives, the catalog's fingerprint (SHA-256, first 12 digits) and what the last build said, as JSON to paste when describing a problem. The map redraws with every better layout and shows its area.
- `js/render.js`: pan/zoom canvas map (drag, wheel, fit) with hover details, item icons on machines, belts and pipes, and the copper wires between poles (`js/layout/wires.js`, shared with the blueprint).
- `js/worker.js`: runs the search in a module Web Worker and posts each better layout; Stop terminates it and the page keeps the best one. With Maximize, it posts each try and each layout that fits.
- `js/city.js`: blueprint strings (and books) decoded, the City Block and its Fixtures read from a blueprint, entity footprints, the Buffer. `js/maximize.js`: Maximize, its tries and the Foretelling (`planner`, also used by the page). `js/layout/place.js`: Placement, Layers (`inLayers`), Parting (`part`: a stack of copies parted round Fixtures), Nooks (`nooksOf`), Making Way (`holes`: the machines Fixtures stand on) and how much of a City Block a design fits (`roomFor`).
- `js/search.js`: the anytime layout search, bottom-up (ADR 0004, 0005). It designs every Sub-Block in Dependency Order (leaves first), then tries Compound Blocks: first each Sub-Block's best design with a few placements (in a City Block, also every Sub-Block's narrowest design together), then each Sub-Block's other designs, then random changes to the best so far (a Sub-Block's design, corridor, gap, link-length weight, a Sub-Block's lift or order). Each candidate is placed, linked, squeezed, powered and checked by `validateBlock`; the best by Starvation, then Recipe Loops fed by train, then output inserters at machines with an Output Drop, then Compactness (`js/layout/score.js`), then entity count is kept. Deterministic for a seed and candidate count.
- `js/design.js`: a Sub-Block's design candidates — v2's variants of its core (rows, shared bands, parallel belts sized by lanes, Belt Merge, pipe rows, outputs given the shared band first), Side and Head-on Belts for single machines, and for huge Sub-Blocks repeated Modules sized about square — ranked by Starvation (inserters, belts, lanes) and area, each routed as a Module when first needed; Breakout's designs, and Making Way's (`leaveOut`: copies without the machines Fixtures stand on, as many standing apart).
- `js/solve.js`: `solve()` runs the search for a candidate or time budget and returns the best Compound Block. `js/bands.js`: Bands — a City Block's Slots (`slotsOf`, `gapsOf`), the chain in Parts packed into them, designed, placed, linked and checked (`bands`).
- `js/chain.js`: Goals + the items chosen to be made here + Recipe Selections → the Production Chain (every step's rate, and the Train Inputs it needs), and the recipe and building options for each item.
- `js/plan.js`: Goals → Sub-Blocks (Count, rates, Byproducts, Fuel, modules). `js/modules.js`: which modules fit a building and recipe, the default ones, and their effect on speed, productivity and power. `js/fluidboxes.js`: which of a building's fluid boxes each fluid of a recipe takes, as the game assigns them — a fluid with a `fluidbox_index` that box alone, the others sharing every input (output) box in recipe order, the first ones a box more where they do not divide evenly (a research center takes arqad honey for py-science-pack-2 at two inputs, flavonoids at the third); a pipe may meet any connection of its fluid's boxes, and a building whose boxes cannot take a recipe's fluids is not offered for it. `js/flows.js`: Dependency Order, Internal Paths, Side Input, Side Output. `js/routes.js`: routes with Belt Merge.
- `js/layout/core.js`: one Sub-Block (or Module) from a variant — rotation, stacked machine rows with shared bands between them, which band and row each belt part and pipe row takes (up to 4 belts per face), Side and Head-on Belts beside single machines, row shift, gaps, pole slots, taps, Output Drops (`dropOf`: a machine's drop tile once rotated; an output belt past them takes them, 90° inserters supporting where the lane may be full, other drop tiles kept clear). A small depth-first search picks inserter columns for one machine period (repeated for every machine) so every machine gets its share of each belt and every belt row stays passable.
- `js/layout/module.js`: routes a core on its own as a Module (west to east, or with chosen parts east to west for alternate Copies), with risers for fluids in several bands, poles to prove it powers, each output inserter's lane (a machine's Output Drop counted like an inserter fixed on its lane), and where a Copy may drop its belt's head or tail.
- `js/layout/compose.js`: Copies of every Module and the global routes (parts chained into belts by capacity and lanes, Internal Paths balanced, pipes one network per route), then links and pipes routed between placed Copies (rip-up and reroute on failure). `js/layout/place.js`: the Placement. `js/layout/compact.js`: the Squeeze, straight pipe runs underground (`buryPipes`), then poles.
- `js/layout/router.js`: A* belts through waypoints and pipe trees; tunnels of any hop length up to the reach (ADR 0003), never interleaved on a line; a waypoint may hold a belt, a tunnel entrance or exit (an exit only where the belt can go on). A pipe tunnel never ends on or passes under a tile that must take a plain pipe (a connection on its pipe row, or where a tap joins it).
- `js/layout/poles.js` (Minimal Pole Placement; poles may stand just north or south of the block), `grid.js`, `validity.js` (the rules every candidate and every test checks, among them only a machine's own output belt on its drop tile).
- `js/inserters.js`: approximate inserter throughput. Hand-size items per swing: a swing turns the hand 180° (90° for a 90° inserter) at the prototype's rotation speed while it extends between its pickup and drop distances; the slower of the two sets its time. Pickup and drop are ignored, so real rates run a few percent lower (fast 2.4/s estimated vs 2.31/s measured).
- `js/sim.js`: Starvation per route in belt order, plus machines whose inserters cannot move their share.
- `js/blueprint.js`: importable blueprint string + JSON, with pole wires, `pickup_position` / `drop_position` vectors on 90° inserters, module requests on machines, display panels marking the train routes and the enable condition of every inserter whose clock has a signal.
- `js/clocks.js`: each inserter's Inserter Clock, from what `js/layout/core.js` records it moves (`role`, `moves`, `sharing`), and every clock of a block.
- `js/catalog-builder.js` + `scripts/build-catalog.mjs`: data dump → catalog. `scripts/py-farms.mjs`: Py farm modules and speeds from the pyalienlife source into the catalog. `scripts/measure.mjs`: samples recipes from the catalog and reports how many solve.
- `scripts/build-sprites.mjs` + `scripts/sprites.mjs`: extract the catalog's icons from the game and the mod zips into `sprites/`, or fill in those it lacks from an icon dump (`--dump`). `scripts/site-sprites.mjs`: the Pages site's icons.

## Known limitations
Measured with `scripts/measure.mjs` (300 sampled Pyanodons recipes at 2–4 machines with their default modules, 3 s search each, vanilla inserter data standing in until the catalog is regenerated): 92.7% solve, 2.8 s a recipe on average (v2: 90.7%, v1: 79.3%). Of the rest, 15 of 22 have more belts than fit around a small machine or no room for their inserters, 4 burn a fuel other than coal, 3 fail on fluids.
- A row of machines reaches at most 4 belts per face; a machine alone in its row also takes Side and Head-on Belts (up to 12 belts around it). Recipes needing more after Belt Merge are rejected with an error, and small machines (3×3 assemblers) with many ingredients often have no room for the inserters their belts need. Py cranes are not used.
- About 1% fail on fluids: a connection boxed in by the machine's other connections and belts that the pipe cannot leave, or a pipe that cannot reach the rest of its network.
- Every machine in a row uses the same inserter columns (one period repeated); a layout that would need different columns per machine is not found.
- Inserter throughput ignores pickup and drop time; the hand size is a setting.
- Output Drops are used on a machine's top and bottom faces (the rotations that put them there): never by a belt along its side. A row's supporting inserters are sized for the machine its belt passes last, so a row whose drops do not fit their lane gets an inserter at every machine; without 90° inserters a machine making more than a lane starves the rest.
- Straight output inserters fill only the far lane, and a drop onto a belt's curve counts on the worse lane. A machine making more than one lane holds (bolts at 600/min on yellow belts) needs a Head-on output or rows facing its belt from both sides; where the pairing of an Internal Path allows neither, it starves.
- An Internal Path feeds each consumer machine from one belt, and each belt from a run of producer machines: when the machines do not split evenly (13 iron-stick machines into 5 belts), some belts bring less than their consumers take. Py small parts at 1200/min with hand size 1 on yellow belts lays out in about 4 s but runs some 1,200/min short (bolts and iron sticks beyond a lane); at 2000/min, about 1,800/min short. Faster belts or a higher hand size make such blocks far easier.
- Splitters are not used: a Head-on input ends its belt, so it serves only where a machine gets a belt of its own; Side Belts serve the machines that share one.
- A Fan-out feeds copies in one column, or Sub-Blocks each taking a Side Input on a route of its own whose belts carry the same items (copies in several columns do not share), and its splitters stand within 8 tiles of the entries they feed (across Sub-Blocks, 8 columns west of them and 4 rows above or below): beside a column of copies against the west edge (its fluids' trunk right before the entries, every belt bound further east passing there) there is often no room, and those copies keep a belt each from the west edge. Placement leaves no room for a Fan-out of its own. On yellow belts, items a copy's band takes 80% of a belt of (Py fertilizer's ash and biomass) save a belt only through joining belts, which rarely route; faster belts take a block far closer to the fewest belts that carry its rate.
- The search is a randomised local search: it finds good layouts, not proven optimal ones, and harder blocks need more search time. Py science pack 2 at 450/min: a starvation-free layout of some 18,900 tiles in about 12 s.
- Belts snaking through the Copies of a stack turn beside it, each in a lane of its own, at most (min(belt reach, pipe reach) − 2) / 2 of them per stack (one on vanilla yellow belts); beyond that the Internal Paths snake first, then outputs, and every Copy gets its own belts for the rest. Copies may stand in several columns, but each column needs its own turning room, so one column usually wins.
- A Py farm for Factorio 2.0 whose modules are all removed is planned at its own speed, though Py's script stops it.
- Machines heated by their fluid's temperature (Py's TURD compost plants) are not supported. Machines join through their fluid boxes only along a row. One byproduct per belt is sorted out.
- Fluids Between in more than two rows joins each fluid's pipe bands in a riser beside the stack, not by a stub of its own per band at the west edge.
- A Recipe Loop's feedback is tapped only off belts its producer's items leave on (to the train or the Sub-Blocks it feeds) that no splitter pair joins; standing on its own the block keeps a row along its top for each feedback, in a City Block it finds its way between the rest, and where it finds none the loop's item comes by train (the layout ranks below one that feeds itself). Fluids in a loop, byproducts a recipe takes back (Py's queens: 1 in, 0.999 out) and loops taking more than they make come by train.
- Icons with several layers (tinted, overlaid) show only their first layer.
- Pole placement is locally minimal (no pole can be removed), not a proven global minimum.
- Custom/mod entity definitions are deferred (see Requirements).
- Bands cuts a City Block at one Fixture column at most and gives each Part one or two rows of a whole design, one Part to a Slot (two of one row one above the other, or two of the Goal side by side; a Slot's rows a Part leaves stay empty); its producer Parts fill their Slots (spare machines run slower); whether its layout routes comes and goes with the Count, so Maximize bisects below the most its Parts hold and tries once two above the best, and may miss a Count that routes. Lines from producers and the Side Inputs' lines share the four columns between the west edge and the west Parts: at 60 Py vrauks-2 paddocks the Side Inputs come in on a belt per Part (15 from the west edge, where four would carry them). The outputs leave on a belt per Part. The search alone has no Fan-outs from producers.
- In a City Block the Side Input always enters on the west, the Side Output leaves on the east; the block is not squeezed, so it packs looser than one standing on its own; a Sub-Block stands in another's Nook only whole (Copies of one Sub-Block are not spread over several Nooks), in Layers only where its column does not fit otherwise, and the Nooks of a block standing on its own are left empty; Sub-Blocks never bend round a Fixture, though a Fixture may stand where a few of their machines would (Making Way: a tenth of a Sub-Block's at most, each built apart; never on a belt or pipe; in Layers a stack of copies in one column parts round them, Parting), so a dense grid of Fixtures (substations every 18 tiles) leaves room mostly for designs that fit between them (Py small parts in 116 × 116: 1575/min there, 3375/min in an empty block): a Sub-Block's machines in one row cannot split its output onto several belts, and copies of a smaller Module cut an Internal Path into belts unevenly where no Two-Way Copy fills them (the Count rounded up leaves machines too slow to fill a belt and a half each), so a busy Sub-Block may have no design narrow and short enough. Large City Blocks of busy machines are limited by their belts: every Py small parts factory takes two and a third yellow belts, and where many belts cross columns of Layers their links take long to route or find no way (Py small parts in 230 × 230: 7200/min, the 14-factory layouts' links from the bolts round the gears and cable to the small parts factories finding none). Maximize passes one number whose designs starve, not two in a row. A City Block's poles are assumed to be one network already. Maximize gives the highest rate the search finds a layout for, not a proven maximum: Filling stops below a quarter machine, and a try that runs out of time counts as not fitting. The Foretelling knows of Fixtures only the tiles they take and how much of the room they break up, and nothing of designs that starve; it learns how loosely a City Block packs from the box round a layout's Sub-Blocks, which in Layers spans nearly all of it, so Maximize may climb a machine a try (Py vrauks among roboports: 31 to 50 paddocks, some 30 s each).
