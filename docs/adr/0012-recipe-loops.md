# A Recipe Loop is sized for its Goal and fed in the block

Py breeds its animals in loops: arqad eggs take arqads, and the arqads come from the eggs (through maggots and nests). The chain used to cut a loop where its walk met an item a step on the way already made: that item came by train. The layout then still fed it from its own Sub-Block, so only a sixth of the arqads made reached the train, and the step had been sized for all of them to.

The chain now keeps such an item in the block. Each step makes its Goal rate plus all its consumers take. With a loop, that is a linear system, R = g + A R, where A says how much of one step's item one of another's takes. A machine's productivity counts. Gaussian elimination solves it, rounded to a billionth so whole machines stay whole. Without a loop, the steps are still summed in turn, consumers first, as before. A loop that takes more than it makes has no solution with every rate positive: its item comes by train, as before, and so does a fluid in a loop.

The flows mark a loop's last link — the one whose walk, from the Goals toward what each takes, meets a Sub-Block still on the way — as its feedback. The feedback is no Internal Path. The Dependency Order, placement and design never see the loop, and the feedback looks to the Sub-Block taking it like a Side Input of its own (never sharing a belt with another).

The layout feeds it from the producer's output belt. The user asked for a splitter whose output priority favours the loop, so it cannot run out. A splitter on the belt's link after its producers sends the feedback off on its priority side, and the rest goes on to the train. The loop is served first. Only what it does not take reaches the train, and a short-running producer starves the train, never the loop.

The feedback comes from the producer, the loop's last step, so usually from the east. It enters the Sub-Block it feeds from the east too: that band runs east to west in its module. A belt coming round to the west side would cross every Side Input arriving there. Placement leaves room east of the producer for the splitters and east of the Sub-Block fed back for the feedback to come down. Standing on its own, the block keeps a row along its top for each feedback to run back west on. The feedback is routed after every other link.

When no splitter finds room, the feedback comes by train, and the station has to loop the item round. Such a layout ranks below one that feeds itself, whatever its Compactness. Starvation still ranks first.

Py arqads at 2 a minute: 12 made, 10 back into the eggs through one splitter, nothing of it by train. A recipe taking four of its own product for five (like kovarex) makes five times its Goal in an 8 × 6 block, its feedback running from the splitter round the machine into its own band.
