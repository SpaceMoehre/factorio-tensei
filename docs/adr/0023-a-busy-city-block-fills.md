# What kept a City Block of busy machines from filling

Py small parts in a 230 × 230 City Block, on yellow belts with fast inserters of hand size 1: Maximize stopped at 5175/min (9 small parts factories), its Compound Block 228 × 167 with 32,000 empty tiles, where the Foretelling said about 13,200/min fits. Every machine of this chain is busy: a bolts factory takes and makes 600 a minute, a belt of iron sticks of its own; a small parts factory takes a whole belt of bolts and one of copper cable. Three causes, each found in a try that should have fitted:

- **Starvation comes and goes with the Count.** At 5362.5/min, 14 bolts factories starved by 27/min; 13 and 15 did not. Iron sticks starved for 9, 11 and 13 small parts factories, not for 10, 12 or 14. Whether a Sub-Block starves depends on how its machines cut into its Internal Paths' belts, not on how many there are. Maximize took the first Count whose designs starved as the highest that could fit, as if more machines always starved more. Now a number whose designs starve bounds the tries only until the whole numbers below it are done; then the one above it is tried, once. Right after the first that starves, the next try is the number just below it; after two in a row, halfway down as before (a machine too busy for its inserters starves at every Count). A try that found no room still bounds everything above it: more machines never take less room. A Sub-Block none of whose designs without Starvation fits the City Block counts as starving: which designs exist comes and goes with the Count too.
- **Stacks taller than the City Block.** Past 13 small parts factories, the bolts' only design without Starvation was one stack, a factory to a row: each takes its iron sticks on a Head-on Belt, and drops its bolts onto the belts above and below it, so each belt brings a small parts factory its 900 bolts from one and a half factories (Two-Way Output). 21 of them stand 231 tiles tall. Copies of one or two factories cut the path's 14 belts unevenly: whole factories, some belts got 600 where 900 were wanted. Now **Two-Way Copies**: a Copy of the fewest machines that fill some of the path's belts whole (3 bolts factories fill 2 belts; 5 iron stick factories the belts of 2 bolts factories; 15 copper cable factories 4), the Copies and the leftover Module together filling at least as many belts as the path has. Each of a Copy's belts out is one of the path's, wanted for the most any of them brings, and runs straight on to its consumers rather than snaking through the other Copies. Copies stand in columns. Single machines in Copies take Side and Head-on Belts where belts above and below cannot feed them, as a whole Module's do.
- **Belts squeezed between two Sub-Blocks of a column.** Layers counted a row for every belt crossing a column, but stacked the column's Sub-Blocks wherever they wanted to stand. Twelve belts of bolts had to pass between the gears and the copper cable through a single row: each layout found no way for the last ones. Now a column of several Sub-Blocks keeps a corridor across it, a row for every belt crossing it, above, between or below them, wherever lies nearest where those belts arrive. With 12 small parts factories the try found a layout after 7 candidates (177 s), not 79 (757 s); with Two-Way Copies too, after 3.

Py small parts in 230 × 230, five minutes a try: 7200/min (12 small parts factories), not 5175/min, in 13 minutes.

What still keeps such a block from filling is its belts. Every small parts factory takes two and a third belts; at 22 of them some 140 belts link the Sub-Blocks, and their links take long to route or find no way.
